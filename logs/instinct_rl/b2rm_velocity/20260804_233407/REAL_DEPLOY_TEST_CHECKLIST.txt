- Keep an emergency stop ready and use physical support for initial tests.

4. Log every 50-Hz policy cycle with one shared timestamp
- command: cmd_vx, cmd_vy, cmd_wz
- estimated motion: vx, vy, wz
- IMU: quaternion, roll, pitch, angular velocity
- all 12 leg joints: q, dq, q_target
- all 12 raw policy actions and clipped policy actions
- all 12 measured or estimated joint torques
- actual Kp and Kd sent to every motor
- raw foot-force values and derived contact states
- policy input min/max, NaN/Inf flags, and history-buffer readiness
- ONNX inference time, control-loop dt, and deadline overruns
- controller phase: stand, handoff, policy, emergency stop
- robot mode, safety/protection state, and error codes

5. Metrics to calculate after each test
- mean and maximum abs(cmd_vx - vx)
- mean and maximum abs(cmd_vy - vy)
- zero-command planar drift and backward-drift bias
- roll/pitch RMS and maximum absolute angle
- per-joint maximum abs(q_target - q)
- per-joint torque peak and sustained high-torque duration
- raw-action and target-limit clipping ratio
- four-foot contact duty factor and diagonal/left-right asymmetry
- control-loop dt mean/max and deadline-overrun count
- number, time, and reason of every safety stop

6. Evidence required for diagnosis
- Save the complete raw log, not only terminal summaries.
- Save a synchronized video of the whole robot.
- Mark the exact video/log time when an anomaly begins, for example:
  "12.4 s: starts drifting backward" or "8.1 s: rear legs soften".
- If available, record an independent velocity reference such as motion
  capture, visual odometry, or measured travel distance. This distinguishes
  policy failure from biased onboard velocity estimation.

7. Training reference targets
Velocity/vx_error: below 0.05 m/s is good; 0.03-0.04 m/s is excellent.
Velocity/vy_error: below 0.03 m/s.
Velocity/zero_command_drift: below 0.02-0.04 m/s.
